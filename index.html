A data story about bushwalking

Australia on Foot

Where do Australians hike, who heads out on the trail, and what makes some of our national parks so much busier than others?

Why it matters

Bushwalking is one of the most popular physical activities in Australia. It costs almost nothing, suits every age group and gets people into the country's national parks.

3

Who goes hiking, and where?

Participation is highest in the cooler south. Tasmanians and Canberrans are the keenest walkers, while Queensland has the lowest rate.

4

Popular national parks

Visitors cluster near the big cities. The Blue Mountains, about two hours from Sydney, attracts more visitors than Kakadu and Uluru-Kata Tjuta put together.

5

When and where people walk

The hiking season flips with latitude. The tropical north is busiest in the dry winter months (May–Sep), while Tasmania peaks in summer.

6

What makes a trail challenging?

Distance alone doesn't make a walk hard. The climb matters just as much. Most day walks are rated Easy or Moderate, so there is a trail for everyone.

7

Key takeaways

  • ⛰️Bushwalking participation is growing across Australia, and the cooler southern states lead.
  • 👥Middle-aged and older Australians walk the most.
  • 📍Visitors concentrate in a handful of parks close to the major cities.
  • ☀️Season and climb shape where and when people choose to walk.

From the Bibbulmun to the Overland Track, Australia's landscapes give everyone a reason to lace up their boots.
