Why it matters

Bushwalking is one of Australia's most popular ways to stay active. It costs almost nothing, suits most ages and gets people into the country's national parks.

The ACT grew the most, up 4.6 percentage points. South Australia was the only one to fall.

2

Who goes hiking, and where?

Tasmanians and Canberrans are the keenest walkers: more than 1 in 4 adults went bushwalking in 2025. In every state except South Australia, women walk more than men.

21.4%

of 35–44 year olds bushwalked in 2025, the highest of any age group.

After 65 the rate halves to 9.6%, even though older Australians have the most free time.

3

Parks on the doorstep

Australia has almost 800 national parks. The biggest are in the remote outback, but the states where parks cover more of the land are also the states where more people walk.

4

The busiest parks are near the cities

NSW national parks recorded a record 65.5 million visits in 2024–25. Nine of the ten busiest are within about two hours of Sydney or Newcastle.

2.4 M

visits to Lane Cove National Park, a strip of bush only 7 km² in Sydney's suburbs.

That is the same number of visits as Kosciuszko, which is almost 1,000 times bigger. Parks close to home get used the most.

5

When is the best time to walk?

The hiking season flips with latitude. Outback and tropical parks are too hot for long walks in summer, so their season is winter. Alpine and Tasmanian parks are best in summer.

6

Australia's great walks

We measured 32 of Australia's long-distance walks, from the 8 km George Bass Coastal Walk to the 1,100 km Heysen Trail. How hard a walk feels depends not only on its length but on how much you climb: the Stirling Range Ridge Walk climbs 85 m for every kilometre, more than 20 times the flat Yuraygir Coastal Walk.

  • Gentle: under 15 m of climb per km. Good for beginners.
  • Hilly: 15–30 m per km. Needs some fitness.
  • Steep: over 30 m per km. For experienced walkers.

Length is measured from each route on OpenStreetMap. Climb is estimated from satellite elevation data sampled every 500 m, so real totals will be a little higher.

7

Key takeaways

  • 🥾Nearly 4 million Australians, about 1 in 6 adults, went bushwalking in 2025, and the number is growing.
  • 🌿Tasmania and the ACT have the most park land and the most walkers.
  • 🏙️The busiest parks are small parks close to the cities.
  • ☀️Pick the season to suit the park: winter in the north and outback, summer in the mountains.

With a national park within reach of most Australians, the easiest walk to start is the one closest to home.

Pick a trail that suits your fitness, go in the right season, and leave no trace.
